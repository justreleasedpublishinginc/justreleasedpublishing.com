About Cory Kaufmann & Just Released Publishing

Just Released Publishing Inc. (JRP Inc.) is an independent publisher based in London, Ontario, Canada, founded by Cory Kaufmann. JRP owns the copyrights, ISBNs, and publishing rights to the Screwed Up series.

My name is Cory Kaufmann, and I'm living an inspiring and transformative life. After spending 14 years in prison for grave mistakes I made as a teen, I have turned my journey into a force for change. I've created a platform for incarcerated individuals to express themselves through art, while increasing participants' motivation, self-esteem, sense of purpose, achievement, and validation, by publishing poetry books written exclusively by inmates.

During my time in prison, I experienced profound personal growth and witnessed it in others. These experiences made me a strong believer in the power of change. While I'm not proud of the mistakes I've made in the past, I am proud of the man I've become — so are my family and friends. Their support — phone calls, letters, visits, confidence, motivation, and love — is a large part of how I got through.

Through poetry, our authors explore the complexities of being incarcerated: the self-sabotage of addiction and its consequences, and decisions that destroy lives and tear families apart. They come to understand that their actions have ripple effects that reach victims, family, and community — a lesson I learned in a Restorative Justice program.

"I want to show society that not all convicts are bad people with bad intentions — they've made mistakes, but they're capable of change."

Our mission is to create a platform for the often-unheard and voiceless, enabling incarcerated individuals to express themselves through art and poetry — sharing their lives, experiences, and emotions with readers. Through this creative expression, we inspire personal growth, emotional healing, and a renewed sense of purpose for individuals behind bars, while fostering hope and delivering engaging content for readers.

The correspondence is the heart of it. The ongoing back-and-forth with authors at every stage of the publishing process is what makes incarcerated writers feel good about themselves. Without it, the claims about the benefits — motivation, self-esteem, purpose, validation — would hold no weight.

The series never glorifies crime. As part of our commitment to neither profit from nor glorify crime, these books contain no details that promote any prisoners' crimes. We do not accept and will not publish any content that in any way glamourizes crime.

What sets JRP apart: the content is written from many cells across the country and curated by someone who spent 40% of his life incarcerated, with deep, firsthand expertise in the correctional system — provincial and federal, super-max to minimum security. Cory built the infrastructure to keep collecting submissions while working a full-time job alongside the series.

Support an Author — Send a Message Inside

A short message from a reader can change an author's week. The authors in these books write without knowing if anyone outside will ever read them. Hearing that a poem landed — that a stranger read it and cared — is a big part of what keeps them writing, and it's the same encouragement the letters in the Voices section above describe.

How it works — nothing goes direct, and nothing is posted automatically:

  • You write it here. Address it to one author or poem, to a volume, or to the authors in general.
  • Cory reads every message first. He vets each one before it goes any further — that oversight is the safeguard.
  • He forwards approved messages inside through his ongoing correspondence with the authors, the same letters that built these books. Institutional mail takes time, so please allow weeks, not days.
  • No reply is promised. Some authors write back through Cory, many can't. Send the message as the gift it is, not a conversation you're owed.

What makes a good message: name the poem or volume that stayed with you, say in a line or two what it did for you, and keep it encouraging and respectful. A few honest sentences beat a long letter.

House rules, so a message can be forwarded: no personal contact details (yours or anyone else's), no offers of money, gifts, or legal help, no questions about anyone's crime or case, and nothing that glorifies crime. Messages that break these rules aren't forwarded.

Calling All Poets — Submit Your Poetry

Every volume of Screwed Up: Penitentiary Poetry is built from submissions by incarcerated writers in Canada. If you write poetry inside — or you're a family member, volunteer, or worker helping someone inside — this is how a poem reaches us.

  • 1Inside? Watch for our posters and memos in your institution — they carry the current call for submissions and mailing instructions. Follow them exactly, and keep a copy of everything you send.
  • 2Helping from outside? Email info.justreleasedpublishing@gmail.com and we'll send you the current instructions for passing work in or sending it on an author's behalf. All website correspondence goes to this inbox.
  • 3Send original poems only — your own work, typed or written clearly. Include the name or pen name you want to be credited under, and a line or two about the piece if you'd like.
  • 4Cory reads everything himself and corresponds with authors through the process. Selection for a future volume is never guaranteed, and Volume 3 is already in production — new submissions are read with future volumes in mind.

What we're looking for: honest poems about your life, your choices, your family, and your change — the real experience of being inside, in your own voice. Through poetry, our authors explore addiction, loss, family, and the ripple effects of their actions on victims, family, and community.

What we will not publish: anything that glorifies or promotes crime, details that promote anyone's crimes, or work that isn't yours. That rule has never changed and it protects every author in the book.

Not sure if a piece fits? Send it and ask. The correspondence matters as much as the selection — it's how authors stay connected to the process at every stage.

Book Club & Classroom Prompts — Discussing the Poems Together

What this is, in full: a free, ready-made set of discussion questions for anyone reading the series with other people — a book club, a classroom, a community group, or a family reading a loved one's copy. Instead of “so… what did everyone think?”, your group gets questions built on the themes the authors themselves write about: being heard, ripple effects, and change.

Who it's for: book clubs looking for a Canadian collection with something real to talk about; teachers and professors who want seminar questions to pair with a review copy and Cory's virtual class Q&A (see For Educators); reading and reintegration groups; and families, for whom these poems often open conversations that are hard to start any other way.

How to use it: pick one volume, choose one theme below, and work through its three or four questions in about an hour. A simple ground rule helps every group: discuss the poems and the ideas — nobody has to share personal history unless they want to. There are no right answers and nothing to submit; the prompts are yours to use, print, or copy into your group's notes.

Voice & Being Heard

For either volume · start here with a new group

  1. Several authors describe writing as the first time they felt heard. What in the poems changes when a writer knows a stranger will read their work?
  2. Pick one poem and read a passage aloud. What does hearing it in a voice — rather than reading it silently — add?
  3. What surprised you most about daily life inside as these poems describe it? What did you expect that turned out to be wrong?
  4. If you could ask one author in this volume one question, what would it be?

Ripple Effects & Responsibility

The series' core idea, learned in restorative justice

  1. The authors write about “ripple effects” — how one decision reaches victims, family, and community. Where do you see that idea working in a specific poem?
  2. How do the poems take responsibility without making excuses? Find a line that does both at once.
  3. These books never glorify crime. How is writing honestly about a life different from glorifying it — and why does that line matter?
  4. Whose perspective is missing from a poem you read — a parent, a victim, a child — and how does that absence shape it?

Change & Hope

Best as a closing session

  1. What does change actually look like in these poems — grand turning points, or small daily decisions? Which feels more true?
  2. Family support runs through the series: calls, letters, visits. What role does being believed in play in the change these writers describe?
  3. Does publishing change what a poem is? What might it do for a writer to hold a book with their name in it?
  4. After finishing the volume: has your thinking about incarceration shifted in any specific way? Name the poem that did it.

What is Screwed Up: Penitentiary Poetry?

Screwed Up: Penitentiary Poetry is a Canadian poetry book series written exclusively by incarcerated people, published by Just Released Publishing Inc. of London, Ontario. Volume 1 was published on December 22, 2021 (103 pages, ISBN 9781738298549) and Volume 2 on December 6, 2023 (183 pages, ISBN 9781738298563). Volume 3 is in production.

Who writes the poems?

Every poem is written by a person incarcerated in Canada, in provincial or federal institutions. Publisher Cory Kaufmann corresponds with the authors, collects their work, and curates each volume. Volume 2 includes 22 contributing authors, and is the volume that includes women, Indigenous authors, and authors of various faiths.

Who is Cory Kaufmann?

Cory Kaufmann is the founder and publisher of Just Released Publishing Inc. He spent 14 years in prison for grave mistakes he made as a teen, and built the Screwed Up series after his release so incarcerated people could publish their poetry. He is based in London, Ontario, Canada, and works full time alongside running the series.

Does the book glorify crime?

No. As a matter of principle the series never glorifies crime: the books contain no details promoting any prisoner's crimes, and Just Released Publishing does not accept or publish content that glamourizes crime of any sort. Through poetry, the authors explore the consequences of their actions — including the ripple effects on victims, families, and communities.
